# f16 weight storage (`ZJB2`) — measured, 2026-09-19

Task `f16w`: can the browser model bundles be halved by storing the f32 network weights as
IEEE-754 halfs? Implementation: `pack-models --f16` writes `ZJB2` (the `ZJB1` envelope, `ZJM1`
members with f32 tensors as f16, round-to-nearest-even; integer tensors and `vr_vec.c` copied
untouched; `Checkpoint::f32` upcasts at load). Host: `dev` (Ryzen 9 9950X3D), release build,
`tests/f16_weights.rs` (every table here is its `--nocapture` output). Commit `f877578`.

**Verdict: the format works and is deterministic, but it does not meet the decoder parity
gate. Recommendation: reject f16 weight storage for the shipped bundles — browser and native
alike — and keep `ZJB1`/f32 as the default (it is unchanged).**

## 2. What f16 does to the weights themselves

`f16_weight_statistics` over the 19 checkpoints of a model-1 bundle (all three operating
points; per-tensor rows in `f16_weights_2026-09-19_stats_by_file.tsv`):

| | count |
| --- | ---: |
| f32 weights stored | 19,965,849 |
| not exactly representable in f16 | 19,963,434 (99.988 %) |
| becomes f16 subnormal (\|w\| < 6.1e-5) | 433,807 |
| flushed to zero (\|w\| ≲ 3e-8) | 430,863 |
| saturated to ±inf (\|w\| > 65520) | 0 |
| max relative rounding error | 1.0 (a flushed weight) |
| max relative error among kept normals | ≈ 5e-4 · \|w\| (half ulp) |

The flush-to-zero mass sits almost entirely in the HOP decoder (`VM_hop/decoder_Y_0.012.pth`
alone: 362,121 of 3.34 M weights, 10.8 %; its max relative error hits 1.0). The HOP synthesis
net carries an unusual population of weights below the f16 subnormal quantum — attention
biases/scales trained small — and f16 deletes them.

## 3. The gate on real reference vectors

`f16_vs_reference_on_real_vectors`: every real vector decoded from a `ZJB2` bundle (including
f16-packed eICCI post-filter banks where the stream selects them) and compared to the
reference decoder's dump — the **same** `≤1 LSB in <1/5000 samples` gate the f32 decoder
passes. All 43 rows: `f16_weights_2026-09-19_real.tsv`.

- **43 of 43 vectors fail the gate.** Differing-sample fractions run 1.13 %–7.95 %
  (allowed: < 0.02 %); every differing sample is ±1 LSB, never more, on real content.
- The drift is continuous, not discrete: max abs difference on `y_hat` ≤ 0.155 and on the
  reconstructed f32 planes ≤ 0.51 (0..255 scale) — f16-rounded weights shift activations
  slightly and the samples near a quantisation boundary flip by one.
- The integer stage is untouched and provably so:
  `f16_bundle_preserves_the_integer_stage` — entropy stage decodes `z_hat`, sigma indices,
  residual symbols and gain indices identical to the f32 bundle (same `vr_vec.c` bytes).
- f32 baseline on the same vectors sits at the gate's noise floor (e.g. 51 of 1,491,840,
  3.4e-5, on `img30_base_off_bpp012` vs 49,380 = 3.3e-2 under f16) — the f16 excess is
  two to three orders of magnitude larger.

## 5. Adversarial synthetic sources

`f16_vs_f32_on_synthetic_sources`: 27 generated sources (7 flat greys, 7 ±1-LSB dither
fields, impulse, max-contrast step edge, 6 saturated primaries, 1 px + 2 px checkerboards,
two ramps, and a 1024² mosaic) encoded by **our** encoder at BOP 0.12/0.25/0.5/1.0 plus SOP
and HOP at 0.5 — 162 streams, each decoded with f32 and f16 weights. Full rows:
`f16_weights_2026-09-19_synthetic.tsv`. The f32 decode is the reference here (its parity to
PyTorch is already established).

- All 162 streams differ; **152 of 162 exceed the 1/5000 fraction gate** (median 0.91 %,
  p90 5.2 %, worst `checker1` Hop 22,173 / 196,608 = **11.3 %**). The 10 passes are the
  degenerate flat cases (`grey000`/`grey255`/`impulse`, ≤ 28 differing samples) where the
  reconstruction is flat whatever the weights.
- On real content the deviation was always ±1 LSB; **adversarial content breaks that**:
  `checker2` reaches max abs diff **20** (10,714 differing samples; the pre-quantisation
  plane diverges by 19.5) and `mosaic1024` reaches 3–12 across operating points — the
  f16 decode is not describable as "same image modulo rounding" on these inputs.
  (Consistent with a small weight rounding — e.g. one flipped to/from zero in a later conv
  layer — shifting a block's colour by more than the output quantiser step.)
- `max_d_yhat` runs 0.025–0.64 and `max_d_plane` up to 19.5: the error is injected inside
  the synthesis network, then amplified by the gain upsampling — not in the entropy stage
  (identical symbols) and not in post-filter quantisation.
- `grey000`/`impulse` BOP at low rate are the *least* affected (6–95 differing samples of
  196,608): near-zero latents simply reconstruct flat, insensitive to weight noise. The
  worst cases are the ones designed to push activations: ±1-LSB noise fields (the networks
  amplify sub-quantum weight differences into visible drift) and checkerboards.

## 6. Determinism under f16

`f16_tiers_and_threads_agree_bit_for_bit`: every available SIMD tier (scalar → AVX-512 on
`dev`) and both thread modes decode `img30_base_off_bpp050`, `img30_simple_off_bpp050` and
`img30_high_off_bpp050` from `ZJB2` to **bit-identical** tensors (132 s). The upcast is pure
integer arithmetic; whatever tier or thread count runs, the weights are the same f32 values.

## 7. Recommendation

**Reject f16 storage for the shipped bundles.** The ~50 % size win (7.3 MB brotli on the
BOP page payload) is real and the machinery is correct — integer stage bit-exact, upcast
deterministic, every tier/thread identical — but the output does not satisfy the parity
contract anywhere: 43/43 real vectors fail by 56–397×, 152/162 synthetic streams exceed
the fraction bound (with >1 LSB deviations the gate would never tolerate on the worst),
and both hybrid splits fail on every vector tried. The failure is not a format bug: it is
what 10-bit-mantissa weights do to this network family — too many weights sit at or below
the f16 subnormal quantum (up to 10.8 % of the HOP decoder flush to zero) and the
synthesis gain stages amplify the residue.

- Browser: keep `ZJB1`. If transfer size must shrink further, the honest next rungs are a
  coarser quantisation study (int8 per-channel would be far worse than this — no need to
  run it to know it fails the same gate by more) or zstd/brotli on the f32 bytes (-9 %).
- Native: same conclusion; 8 MB extra is not worth parity.
- The `ZJB2` code path stays in the tree (it is a correct, tested, deterministic format
  and `--f16` is additive) — usable wherever the parity contract does not apply
  (size-constrained previews, perceptual-quality-only consumers). It is not used by any
  shipped artefact.
